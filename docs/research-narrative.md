# Research narrative

My long-term goal is to understand how genetic variation and environmental conditions jointly shape complex traits in perennial crops, and to translate that understanding into strategies for improving fruit quality, disease resistance, and environmental adaptation.

Using citrus as a primary system, I connect genetic mapping, regulatory and evolutionary genomics, multi-environment phenotyping, and experimental interpretation. My preferred role is computationally centered but biologically integrated: I aim to discover genetic signals, build interpretable models, and prioritize mechanisms while working closely with experimental and field collaborators to design decisive validation.

My current doctoral-training goals are to deepen quantitative and population genetics, genotype-by-environment analysis, genomic prediction, regulatory-network integration, and interpretable computation for image-derived and multi-omics phenotypes.
